Hourly generation construction (lib/calculator/energy-model.ts)
Use 2027 as the fixed representative calendar: 8760 one-hour bins. Each month
repeats its mean solar day. Solar position uses latitude 13.75 degrees as an
explicit reference, declination 23.45*sin(2*pi*(284+dayOfYear)/365), and hour angle
15*(hourMidpoint-12) degrees. Day-of-year is the 15th of each month in a non-leap
year. Generation is zero before 06:00 and from 18:00 solar time and whenever solar
elevation is non-positive. This is not a civil-time sunrise/sunset forecast.
Irradiance weight = .7*max(0,sunVector dot panelNormal) +
.3*max(0,sunUp)*(1+cos(panelTilt))/2. The 70/30 split is an authored approximation,
not a measured diffuse fraction or a validated transposition model. There is no
cloud variability or direct/diffuse meteorological input.

Normalize each reference month's weights (15-degree south-facing panel) to
1398.23*monthlyShare per kWp. Compute the chosen panel geometry against the same
reference normalization; never renormalize the chosen geometry to erase its
effect. Horizontal panels have tilt=0 and are exactly independent of azimuth.
Azimuth uses compass degrees N=0,E=90,S=180,W=270. Roof slope and panel tilt are
different quantities. The engine may translate a pitched flush-mounted roof to
a stated panel plane; a flat roof requires an explicit mounting assumption.
Apply the entered shade factor and generation degradation once, then cap each
hour's AC energy to inverterACkW*1h. The PVGIS production reference already includes
its configured14% and modeled losses; no second generic inverter/temperature/
wiring/system-loss percentage is deducted. Other panel tilts/azimuths use an
unvalidated analytical adjustment, not a fresh PVGIS calculation. Model hourly
shapes are synthetic disaggregation, NOT PVGIS hourly weather/production records.
Additional modeled clipping is reported separately; real clipping needs weather
and inverter data. Bifacial gain is not added to catalogue bifacial modules.

Demand construction and conservation
Each supplied month stays exactly at its kWh baseline before future additions.
Absent measured profiles, daytime answers allocate residual baseline energy to
08:00–18:00: very-low .12, low .25, moderate .40, high .55, very-high .70.
These shares and intra-window noon/evening weight curves are authored assumptions,
not appliance telemetry or Thai population estimates. Weekday/weekend answers
use their own shapes; the same total daily residual is used across day types.
Public holidays, occupancy changes and seasonal appliances are not inferred.

Existing AC, pump and EV schedules describe energy ALREADY INSIDE the bill.
Their calculated scheduled kWh are subtracted from each month's baseline; only
the residual follows the generic shape. If scheduled energy exceeds bill kWh,
scale all existing schedules proportionally down to that month's bill and expose
a diagnostic. No negative residual or bill double counting is possible.
AC/pump kWh = average electric-input kW * units * operating hours on selected days.
AC input power should reflect cycling; cooling capacity/BTU is not input power.
Fractional hours and midnight wrap are included as one-hour bin overlaps.
EV monthly kWh is wall charging energy; multiply by home-charging fraction. Home
charging's daylight fraction is spread 09:00–17:00 and its remainder 20:00–06:00.
No charging loss is added again to wall-energy input. Energy/charging power and
sub-hourly coincidence are not independently modeled.

Only quantified future increments add kWh. Each includes startMonth 1–12 and
startYearOffset (0=current scenario year), then persists from that month. Future
EV home fraction/timing and AC/pump input power/count/hours are handled explicitly.
A future-load checkbox by itself adds no generic bonus or inferred consumption.
Lifetime calls reuse the same representative calendar to isolate degradation,
load changes and economics from arbitrary future weekday/year differences.

For each hour: selfUse=min(demand,generation), import=demand-selfUse,
surplus=generation-selfUse. Thus demand=selfUse+import and
generation=selfUse+surplus for every interval, month and year. No battery exists
in this model. Surplus is physical energy; financial export eligibility/payment
must be handled separately. Degradation changes generation before hourly matching,
not the monetary savings or self-consumption total after calculation.
